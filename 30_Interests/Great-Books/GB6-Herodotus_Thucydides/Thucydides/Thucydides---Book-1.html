Chapter 1

(The State of Greece from the earliest Times to the Commencement of the Peloponnesian War)

  • As per Thucydides, the war between Athens and Sparta broke out because Athens was becoming more powerful. He says that when any new country becomes more powerful than an existing country, it creates conflict and war.
  • The more fertile a country, the more immigration into that country. Also in that country some people might hoard more wealth than others. If a country is rocky and generally infertile, there is little immigration, and general peace.
  • Athens was a rocky country, so it enjoyed peace for a long time. But as there was conflict in other countries, people started moving to Athens. Since it began to have too many people, eventually people migrated out and created colonies in Ionian cities which in turn increased Athens’ influence.
  • All of Greece was not united or claimed common ancestry in the past. Each island or portions of land were managed by tribes. Thucydides says even during the time of Homer, the lands were called by different names. There was no mention of ‘barbarian’ in Homer’s books because there was no such distinction then.
  • Hellas and his descendants unified most of the tribes and they collectively began to be called Hellenes (Greeks). Minos was the first person to have established a navy and curbed piracy.
  • In the past piracy and plunder were not viewed as deeds of disgrace but of might and strength and people used to glorify it. But in that mode of life, everyone had to carry weapons on them daily to protect themselves. Athens was the first to lay down their weapons and have an easier mode of life.
  • As piracy slowed down, big towns with fortified walls, started coming up on shores (when piracy was prevelant, people built towns away from the shores)
  • With time, countries built stronger navies. During the Trojan war, Agamemnon had the largest fleet and hence wielded more power. The Trojan war would have ended much sooner if the Greek fleet had all the provisions they needed. Since they didn’t they had to farm or plunder after they reached Troy to sustain themselves.
  • With settlement and wealth, more tyrants came up in places. There were wars to consolidate power. Thucydides says that for over 400 years, Athens and Sparta had a form of government without the need for tyrants.
  • The first war between the Medes and Athenians took place in Marathon. Ten years later, there was another war, where Athens was burnt and the people became a naval power. They defeated the Persians at Salamis. During this time, the Spartans were united with Athenians. But after the war, there was a division among the Greeks - on one side, people who fought against the Persians and on the other who aided the Persians. After the war, the Spartans were on the side against Athens.
  • Thucydides says people believe tradition that is handed over to them without critical thinking. The notion that Spartan kings had two votes is false.
  • He says he ensures that there is truth in his history. He either hears the speakers directly or interviews them later to make sure what he is writing down is true. The absence of romance or poetry might make it dull but it is a true account.
  • He ends chapter 1 by saying that the real cause of the Peloponnsian war was the rise of Athens.

Chapter 2

(Causes of the War—The Affair of Epidamnus The Affair of Potidaea)

  • Epidamnus was a colony of Corcyra which in turn was an offshoot of Corinth.
  • Epidamnus becomes populous and wealthy and there is a lot of infighting. At one point, the people drive the nobles out of the city. The nobles join the barbarians to attack the city. Seeing the city in ruins, the people ask Corcyra to help. They refuse.
  • People go to Corinth and they accept to help. Corcyra gets alarmed when Corinth steps in to help. They besiege the city and ask Corinth to withdraw.
  • In the ensuing battle, Corcyraeans completely defeat the Corinthians and their allies. They also plunder some ally cities.
  • Corinthians reach out to Athenians for help. Corcyraeans also do the same.
  • Corcyraean Speech
    • They once thought isolationism was wise, but now admit it was folly and weakness.
    • Three benefits - 1. Moral highground - Athens will be helping a victim against an unjust agressor. 2. Gratitude - Helping Corcyra creates a debt of honor that Athens can use in the future. 3. Naval Supremacy - Corcyra has the second largest fleet. If Athens takes them in, Athens becomes the largest force.
    • The war with Sparta or Corinth is inevitable - 1. Sparta is jealous of Athens, 2. Corinth is attacking Corcyra to clear the way to attack Athens later. 3. If Athens waits, they will have to attack the larger combined forces of Corinth and Corcyra later.
    • Moral justification - The treaty between Athens and Sparta allows other states to remain neutral or choose one of them. Though Corinth is their mother city, since it didn’t treat the colony (Corcyra) well, the bond is broken and they are free.
    • Summary - There are only three great navies - Athens, Corcyra, and Corinth. So it is wise for Athens to join forces with Corcyra.
  • Corinthian Speech
    • Corcyra stayed neutral and isolated for their own selfish interests so that they could plunder passing ships.
    • They’re asking for negotiation now when they realized that Corinth is gathering allies.
    • Corinth clarifies that Corcyra is their colony and colonies are not meant to be slaves and Corcyra telling everyone that Corinth expects them to be, is false. Corinth only expects proper respect which they get from other colonies.
    • Corinth asks Athens to not interfere given example of Samian rebellion. When Samias rebelled against Athens, Corinth voted against helping the rebels, saying that it is better to not interfere in Athens’ affairs. If Athens breaks this and support Corcyra, then in the future more states could take sides when there is an internal fight between states.
    • Corinth refutes the Corcyraean claim that war is inevitable. They allege that Corcyra is causing the war and Athens joining to help Corcyra will lead to war.
    • Corinth reminds Athens that they had supplied ships to Athens during their war with Aegina.
  • (The flaw in the Corinthian argument is that they themselves interfered in Corcyra’s internal affars when Epidamnus which was a Corcyran colony rebelled against it)
  • After listening to both sides, Athenians decide to help Corcyra with some conditions. The support would only for defense and not offense. They send 10 ships to Corcyra.
  • Corinthians go back and prepare 100+ ships to attack Corcyra.
  • Corcyraeans prepare to defend by getting 100+ ships ready.
  • During the attack, the right side which had the Athenian ships is able to hold off the Corinthians but the left side is defeated. Though Athenian ships try to come to the rescue, they have to retreat. Later in the night 20 more Athenian ships join. Corinthians withdraw. Both sides claim victory.
  • Potidaea was a Corinthian colony but a tributary ally of Athens. Potidaea revolts against Athens under the leadership of Aristeus. Athens defeats them and besiege the city in spite of the city getting support from Corinth. The siege of Potidaea becomes the main cause of the start of war.

Chapter 3

(Congress of the Peloponnesian Confederacy at Lacedaemon)

  • Sparta calls for an assembly and in the assembly, many cities denounce Athens for being cruel and authoritarian. The Corinthians speak last -
  • The Corinthian Speech
    • The Corinthians give a scathing speech against Lacedaemonian inaction to Athenian aggression. They complain about Athens support of Corcyra and siege of Potidaea.
    • They say Athenians are ambitions and want to keep expanding, but Lacedaemonians are not and want to just hold on to what they have.
    • Athenians are innovative, daring, quick while the Lacedaemonians are not.
    • The Corinthians ask Spartans to stop procrastinating and give up their old fashioned ways. They say that if Spartans don’t standup to their main rival, then Corinth would have to choose other allies.
  • The Athenians Envoys’ Speech
    • Hearing the Corinthians, Athenian envoys request permission to talk.
    • They point out how much they sacrificed for the sake of all Greeks in the fights against the Persians first at Marathon with Darius and then the battle at Salamis with Xerxes. The enemy went away because of Athenian bravery. Their city was burnt to ground but they faced the enemy head on.
    • Their empire grew after the war naturally due to others looking up to them for leadership. It grew honorably and peacefully. Many times they stepped in to help due to Spartan inaction.
    • They always acted with justice and moderation than other powers.
    • They remind Sparta that if Sparta were to take up Athens’ position, they would also be hated. In fact they might be hated more because they are stricter. It is the norm for leaders to be hated. Athenians tell Spartans to consider the matter and decide carefully.
  • Archidamus (Spartan King)‘s Speech
    • Archidamus speaks with a lot of wisdom. He advises caution.
    • Those who long for war actually lack experience of the brutality of war.
    • Going to war against Athens is not the same as war with their neighbors. Athens has naval supremacy and is financially stronger. War costs money.
    • Archidamus says - let’s use diplomacy first. Send ambassadors who can calm the situation without been too aggressive or too submissive.
    • If you attack and destroy something, there is no leverage, there is no incentive on the other party’s end to yield which in turn makes peace tough to achieve.
    • People criticize Sparta for being slow and procrastinating. But in truth, they are practicing moderation, self-control and discipline.
    • True strength comes from practical discipline, respect for law, and preparing for an opponent based on the assumption that their plans are sound and not on the hope that they will make mistakes.
  • Sthenelaidas (the Ephor)‘s Speech
    • He says he doesn’t fully understand Athenians’ speech. He says they did fight well against the Persians but after that they have behaved badly with Sparta’s allies.
    • He says Athens has wealth, ships and horses, but Sparta’s duty is to protect its allies. He says prolonged debate, legal arbitration, etc., are useless, the damage done by real action should be met with swift and decisive counter action.
    • He calls for a vote to preserve honor, prevent further Athenian expansion, protect allies.
  • They take a vote and the majority decide in the favor of war against Athens.

Chapter 4

(From the end of the Persian to the beginning of the Pcloponnesian War —The Progress from Supremacy to Empire)

  • After the Persians were defeated, Athens set out to rebuild their city (since it was burnt to the ground by Xerxes) and their navy.
  • Seeing the speed at which Athens was getting stronger, Sparta and their allies advice them to not build a wall since fortified city could be used as a base by Persians if they attack in the future. Sparta assures that they will always be there to offer protection on land.
  • Themistocles, the famous Athenian general feels otherwise. He wants Athens to be an independent power. Also in the past, Sparta didn’t arrive at the right time to help Athens. He doesn’t say no to Sparta. He says he will go to Sparta and talk through everything. Before he goes he instructs everyone in the city - men, women, and children - to help build a wall with any of the material they could find - burnt material, tomb stones, etc.
  • Thucydides mentions that even during his time he could see the unevenness in the wall in places since it was done in a hurry and using different types of materials.
  • Themistocles goes over to Sparta and stalls the meeting by making many excuses. When he gets word that the wall is in a good shape, he meets the Spartans and tells them that they have built a wall and should be treated as equal power to Spartans since they can now defend themselves.
  • One of the reasons for the shift of leadership from Sparta to Athens was the fall of grace of the famous Spartan leader Pausanias. After the war with Persians, he becomes arrogant, and tyrannical. This alienates many Ionian allies.
  • Sparta recalls Pausanias to face charges and don’t appoint another commander and the void is filled by Athens. Athens captures many of the Persian-held cities along Ionia.
  • Athens collects financial tribute from the allies that it protects. Most allies preferred to pay money rather than provide ships and soldiers.
  • Athens uses the money to build a massive navy and this increases their power. They also make it tough for an ally to leave their league. It was tough for anyone to revolt since Athens had the larger army. If anyone did, it was suppressed. Thucydides says “Athens was severe and exacting”.
